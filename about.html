Project notes

How I made this

An honest accounting of an AI-paired project. Who I am, what I'm not, what I built, what Claude built, and how long it actually took. I'm writing this so the project can be evaluated on what it really is.

01 What I am, and what I'm not

I am not a software developer. I have not worked as one, I do not have a CS degree, and I would not pass a coding interview. Before this project I had not written a Node server, deployed a database, or used GSAP or Rive.

What I do have is a working sense for product, animation, and taste. I know when something feels good and when it feels off. I can write a clear brief. I can read code well enough to follow what it's doing and push back when it's wrong. I have been using Claude daily, across surfaces, for long enough to have a working method for pairing with it.

That mix — product instinct + AI fluency, without engineering training — is what built this site. It's not a hidden detail. It is the project.

02 What is mine

Every decision about what this site should feel like is mine. The premise (a vote site that argues against Brady), the candidates, the gag of the shocked reaction, the choice to scroll-drive the animation rather than autoplay it, the puppet dance, the tone, the pacing — that's the work I did before any code was written, and it's the work I kept doing every day after.

The art direction is mine. The brief that went to the designer (twelve named puppet layers, head turns, hands open and closed, hero composition with throne and crown) is mine. The decision that Brady should look shocked rather than sad when "NOT" drops in is mine. The candidates' personality treatments — lightning for Rivers, hammer effects for Page, party lights for the Brady easter egg — are all mine.

None of that came from Claude. Claude wrote a great deal of code in service of those decisions.

03 What I did, what Claude did

Five concrete examples of how the work actually divided. Each is a real moment from the build, not a generalization.

The "NOT" drop in the hero

Me

Decided the page should open with "Tom Brady is the GOAT" and then, on first scroll, the user should see the word NOT drop in. Decided Brady should react. Decided the reaction should be shocked, not sad.

Claude

Wrote the GSAP timeline, the cross-fade between the two Brady images, the strike-through-on-"the" line, the mobile-specific timing tweaks, and the GPU pre-warm fix when the first scroll on phones felt sluggish.

The puppet

Me

Decided there should be a dancing Brady puppet, that the dance should play in time with scrolling, and that fast scrolling should fire a hidden celebration easter egg. Wrote the layered design brief for the designer.

Claude

Wrote the Rive runtime integration, debugged the file format and animation-name mismatches, fixed the canvas re-sync when the drawing surface drifted, and wrote the velocity-detection logic that triggers the celebration branch.

The vote, the database, the leaderboard

Me

Decided there should be a real shared vote, that Brady should always be pinned to the bottom of the leaderboard regardless of his score, and that voting for Brady should redirect to a related joke site instead of recording a count.

Claude

Wrote the Express server, the Postgres schema, the POST /api/vote upsert, the rate limiter, the cache headers that fixed mobile and desktop showing different totals, and the optimistic-UI pattern with retry-on-failure.

Mobile

Me

Tested the site on my phone obsessively. Reported, in plain language, what felt wrong: the first scroll has resistance, the second one doesn't; the puppet gets cut off; the leaderboard clips below the screen.

Claude

Diagnosed each of those reports as a specific technical cause (URL-bar viewport jitter, GSAP first-tween compilation cost, GPU-layer promotion timing) and wrote the fixes. Many of those fixes are commented in the source so the next reader sees the symptom and the cause together.

This page

Me

Pushed back on a first draft that was too marketing, too enthusiastic, and made up percentage breakdowns. Asked for a rewrite that leads with AI transparency and is honest about my ability.

Claude

Wrote the structure, the prose, the CSS, and the citation footer below. I edited heavily.

04 The Claude surfaces I used

"Built with Claude" is too vague to be useful. I worked across three Claude surfaces, each doing different work.

Claude Code
in the terminal

The actual code editor. Read files, edited files, ran commands, deployed to Railway, debugged production bugs by tailing logs. The vast majority of the codebase was written here through a back-and-forth of briefs from me and edits from Claude.

Claude on the web
claude.ai

Planning and design conversations — what should this section feel like, how should the vote flow, what would a real reader notice. Briefs got refined here before I took them into the terminal. Also where I drafted the brief for the designer.

Claude in Chrome
browser extension

On the deployed site itself — live audits of layout, scroll behavior, animation feel. Useful for "this looks broken in Brave on Windows" diagnostics that the local dev environment couldn't reproduce.

05 How long this took

Three months, working evenings and weekends around a full-time job. Not a weekend project. Not "vibe-coded in an afternoon." Three months of iteration, including:

  • About a week getting the original hand-illustrated assets ready and converting .ai files to layered SVG.
  • About two weeks rigging the Brady puppet in Rive once the layered art was in.
  • The hero scroll sequence took the better part of a month to get to the version on the page now.
  • The vote backend (Express + Postgres on Railway) was about four days of false starts before it worked end to end.
  • Mobile took longer than everything else combined. Easily four weeks of incremental fixes.

I am not counting the amount of Claude wrote in lines of code, because that number is meaningless. The real measure is: this is a project I could not have built alone in any amount of time, and it is also a project no AI built without me.

06 What's actually on the site

Four animated moments, in order:

  • Hero impact. "NOT" drops in red, "the" gets struck through, Brady cross-fades to a shocked photo. Driven by GSAP ScrollTrigger.
  • Accolades pin. Championship rings, MVPs, Super Bowl wins stamp in one at a time as the user scrolls a pinned section.
  • Puppet section. Cartoon Brady dances on a Rive timeline that follows scroll. Fast scrolling triggers a hidden celebration branch.
  • Vote and results. Real votes, recorded in Postgres, rendered as a leaderboard with animated bars. Brady is pinned at the bottom regardless of score.

07 What is still imperfect

A senior engineer would have written less code than I have, and more defensively. There are likely edge cases I have not seen. The CSS in index.html is one large file because I never split it, and the JavaScript is plain ES5-style because that is what I could review confidently. I do not have automated tests. I do not have a build step. The error handling on the vote endpoint is the minimum that worked.

None of those choices are clever decisions. They are limits of what I could verify myself. Where I could not read the code, I refused to ship it.
